Picks

The NFL picks the daily run makes before kickoff, and how they've done, ready to paste into a group chat. Chances are the model's; fair odds are what those chances are worth with no book margin, so a leg is only worth playing where the book pays more. On a day with two or more games there is also one big parlay across them, the likeliest leg of each game (the likeliest two on a day of four games or fewer): a long shot more than a pick.

For every NBA game: four singles, a same-game parlay and the two likeliest PRA leaders, chosen the way the NFL's picks are, and for the whole day one big parlay with one to three legs from each game (twelve at most). Each day has a tab: a game's picks stay up on its day and turn into its results after it, and DraftKings posts player props on game day, so tomorrow's games fill in that morning. A single's chance is DraftKings' price with its margin taken out (on the NBA props graded so far that has predicted better than the model); the model's own figure sits beside it.

How these are chosen

Singles. A moneyline or a player's over / "N+" line (points, rebounds, assists, threes, the sums of them, a double-double) for a player who plays most of the game (24+ minutes when he plays, in 90%+ of the simulations) and has won that line in at least 6 of his last 10 games (so a big who seldom shoots threes does not get a "1+ threes"; this keeps the list to what a player usually does, it does not make picks land more often), whose DraftKings price makes it 58-72% likely once the book's margin (about 5.5%) is taken out, and where the model agrees: no more than 5 points above the chance in the price (a model far above the book is far more often wrong than right) or 7 below it. Unders, steals and blocks are left out: they turn on a few minutes more or less. Best chance first, at most one line about the game, one leg a player and two of any one market, four a game (fewer when DraftKings has not posted the props yet, which it does on the day).

Why the book's chance and not the model's. On the 3,865 NBA prop lines graded so far the book's own chance predicted better than the model's, and giving the model any weight in a mix only made it worse. Where the model was 5+ points above the book the over landed 46% of the time; the model had said 58%. So the number on a pick is what DraftKings' price says, and the model is the second opinion that has to agree. It also means there is no edge claimed: every pick pays less than its chance is worth, by the book's margin.

Same-game parlay. The three of the game's twelve likeliest legs that land together most often: their chances multiplied, times how much more often they landed together in the same simulated game than they would if unrelated, so a team's win and its star's points are not treated as unrelated. Fair odds are what that chance is worth with no margin; a book prices a same-game parlay as one bet, so what it would pay is not known here: compare it with the price in your slip.

Parlay of the day. One parlay across the day's games, one to three legs from each game and twelve legs at most: the likeliest pick from each game first (the twelve likeliest games when the day has more), then, while there is room, a second and a third leg for the games, each only if the simulation does not have it at odds with the game's other legs. Its chance is the games' chances multiplied, and a game's legs count how often they landed together. With one leg a game DraftKings' price is the legs' prices multiplied ("DK about"); with more from a game the book's price is not known. It is remade at each update until the day's first tip-off, then kept as it was, and lost as soon as one leg is (a player who does not play is dropped from it, as a book would). Every leg adds the book's margin (about 5.5%), so the longer it is the more of its value it gives up: a long shot for fun, not an edge.

Tracking. Track on a single, a same-game parlay or the day's parlay, or Track all for the day, puts it in My picks on the Parlays page: each is its own entry there, graded from the box scores and counted, with the chance shown here (DraftKings' price, margin out). It only follows a pick; nothing is bet. A pick already tracked is not added twice.

PRA leaders. The share of the simulated games in which a player had the most points + rebounds + assists of anyone in the game (a tie is shared), beside his projected total (the range is the middle 80% of his simulated games), his last five games and DraftKings' PRA line with the model's chance of the over. This one is the model alone and has not been checked against past games yet; "How they did" keeps count.

A few days of results say little either way: four singles at 63% each all land about one game in six.
